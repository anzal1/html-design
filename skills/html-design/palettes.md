# Palette and mood reference (optional)

Use this only when the user asks for help choosing colours or a direction, or gives
no taste signal at all and wants suggestions. These are starting points, not rules.

## Mood technique

Name a physical setting the product could live in, then take each colour from a real
object in that scene (a harbour at dawn: wet rope, fog, signal-buoy red). Judge the
result by eye afterwards; a good story does not guarantee a good-looking palette.

Other good moods to draw from: sun-bleached, overcast, inky, arid, foggy, subterranean,
tidal, pastoral, chalky.

## Combinations that often read tired or muddy

Cream with terracotta-family reds and oranges; cream with neon; navy or charcoal with
electric purple, lime or teal; plain white with dusty earth tones; a tinted ground
with a very saturated accent. Fine when the user wants them.
