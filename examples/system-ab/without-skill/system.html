Brook design system, part 1 of 3

Soft surfaces, clear water.

Brook is a fictional hydration app. Its language is neumorphic: one soft surface, light from the top left, controls pressed out of it or into it. The usual weakness of that style is low contrast, so every token below has a job in fixing it.

Accessibility rules baked in

Shadows never carry meaning alone

Every control also has a 1.5px --edge border at 3:1 or better against the surface. Shadows add feel, not information.

Focus is a two-tone ring

--focus-ring is a 2px halo plus a 3px ring (deep blue in light, yellow in dark). It reads on raised, sunk and filled surfaces.

State is also shape

Selected segments get an accent underline, toggles get a tick, errors get an icon and text, disabled gets dashed borders.

44px targets, reduced motion

--tap is 2.75rem. Water waves and loaders stop under prefers-reduced-motion.

Elevation

Raised means actionable or a container. Sunk means input, track or well. Pressed buttons flip from raised to inset. Dark mode swaps the highlight to a lighter navy rather than white.

Motion

Short and soft, like a surface giving under a finger. Water is the only thing that moves on its own, and it stops for reduced motion.
